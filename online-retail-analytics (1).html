01 / OVERVIEW

Project Overview

This project analyzes 12 months of transactional data from a UK-based online retailer selling gift and household items, mainly to wholesale customers across Europe. The goal was to turn raw invoice-level data into a clean dataset, a sales dashboard, and a customer-retention model the business could actually act on.

The work covers four stages: data cleaning, exploratory sales analysis, RFM-based customer segmentation, and churn prediction, benchmarking five classification algorithms to find the most reliable one.

03 / DATA CLEANING

Cleaning Pipeline

The raw data had duplicate rows, missing product descriptions, missing customer IDs, and cancelled or invalid orders (negative quantities, zero prices). Each was handled in sequence before building the sales table:

01
Removed exact duplicate rows
5,268 duplicate invoice lines dropped — 541,909 → 536,641 rows.
02
Dropped rows with missing description
1,454 rows had no product description — 536,641 → 535,187 rows.
03
Investigated cancellations
10,624 rows had negative quantity; 9,288 were proper "C"-prefixed cancellations, 1,336 were manual adjustments/write-offs (damages, checks, samples).
04
Dropped missing CustomerID, kept real sales
Removed 133,583 rows with no CustomerID, then kept only Quantity > 0 and UnitPrice > 0 — 392,692 clean sales transactions.
05
Engineered TotalPrice
TotalPrice = Quantity × UnitPrice, exported as the clean dataset used for all downstream analysis.
04 / ANALYSIS

RFM Segmentation & Churn Prediction

Using the clean 392,692-row sales table, an RFM table (Recency, Frequency, Monetary) was built per customer, then K-Means clustering separated 4,338 customers into two groups:

ClusterCustomersAvg RecencyAvg FrequencyAvg Monetary
Regular customers4,31293 days3.9£1,544
High-value customers266 days66.4£85,826

Customers inactive for more than 90 days were labeled churned (1,449 of 4,338, ~33%). Five classifiers were trained on Frequency and Monetary to predict churn, and compared on the held-out test set:

ModelAccuracyPrecisionRecallF1
Naive Bayes52.3%0.410.980.58
Random Forest71.4%0.590.490.53
AdaBoost — best F173.5%0.610.570.59
Gradient Boosting70.5%0.580.430.49
XGBoost70.1%0.560.460.51

AdaBoost gave the best balance of precision and recall, and was selected as the final churn model.

06 / KEY INSIGHTS

Key Insights

01

UK-dominated revenue. ~84% of sales come from the UK, leaving the business exposed if that single market softens.

02

Long-tail product mix. No single SKU drives sales; revenue is spread across thousands of low-to-medium sellers.

03

Strong seasonality. Sales climb sharply from September and peak in November, pointing to holiday-driven demand.

04

Revenue concentration risk. The top customer alone accounts for more sales than the next four combined.

05

A small, high-value segment exists. 26 customers (0.6% of the base) buy far more often and spend ~55x the average — worth a dedicated retention strategy.

06

Churn is predictable, not perfectly. AdaBoost identified churned customers with 73.5% accuracy and a 0.59 F1 score, enough to flag at-risk accounts for outreach.
